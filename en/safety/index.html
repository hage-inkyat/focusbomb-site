We designed safe release before strong restriction.

What it does and doesn't do

Focus Bomb uses Apple's Screen Time API to broadly restrict supported apps and websites. It does not make your entire iPhone unusable. Due to iOS system constraints, phone calls, emergency features, and some system screens may remain available regardless of what any app tries to restrict.

We don't overstate this. We never claim Focus Bomb "fully locks everything but phone calls," is "impossible to unlock," or is "100% safe." It provides broad restriction within iOS's real constraints, and we say so plainly.

Absolute maximum end time

Before you start a session, you see both the planned end time and the worst-case absolute maximum end time. It's calculated as min(start + planned duration × 3, planned end + 120 min), and even wrong-answer penalties can never push your release past that time.

Emergency release

An emergency release button is always visible during a session and in the Warning Room. Hold it for 7 seconds and the shield clears immediately — no Face ID, passcode, network connection, or ad view required. Using it sets that session's Focus Score to 0 and excludes it from official rankings.

Fail-open by design

Focus Bomb always resolves toward releasing you, never toward "keep restricting just in case," when any of the following happens:

  • Corrupted local session data or a failed integrity check
  • A failure reading the signing key from the shared Keychain
  • Loss or revocation of Screen Time permission
  • A detected device clock change (no extension is granted; the session is marked unverified instead)
  • A force-quit or device restart, once the app detects the planned time has already passed at next launch

If our servers, ads, or billing go down

Focus Bomb's release logic is independent of our servers, ads, purchases, Game Center, and team features. If any of those fail, your session continues locally on-device and still releases on schedule.

Team safety design

Even in Team features (when enabled), no host or teammate can change how long your own device stays locked. Every device releases independently.

The 60-second safety test

On first use, after a major OS update, after re-authorizing Screen Time, or after an implementation-version change, a 60-second safety test is required: the shield is actually applied, you confirm supported apps are restricted, then return to Focus Bomb and confirm release. You cannot start a real session until this test passes.

When we recommend against using it

  • While driving
  • During a medical, caregiving, or childcare emergency
  • During a disaster or evacuation
  • Whenever you need transit tickets, Wallet, payments, maps, or ID verification
  • If you haven't confirmed another way to be reached in an emergency

Phone calls and Emergency SOS

Regular phone calls remain available under iOS's own design even while shielded. Because real-device behavior depends on Apple's own Screen Time implementation, we still ask you to confirm an alternate way to be reached before starting (see the pre-session checklist).

Reporting an issue

If you find a puzzle defect (multiple apparent solutions, contradictory hints) or a safety concern, use the report button on the results screen or contact us via the Support page.
